MOTIVATION

The output looks better.
The encoder can still lose the differences.

In a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture, an encoder maps observations to H, and a projector maps H to Z. Prediction and control depend on those representations distinguishing different states.

From initialization, a component shared by different inputs dominates H. Batch normalization removes the batch mean inside the projector. Losses after the projector therefore cannot correct a common shift in H.

The projector can amplify existing differences.
It cannot recover information the encoder has lost.

METHOD

Retain the differences.
Pass them on. Correct only when needed.

Supply And Demand Loss matches what the output demands to what the encoder can retain and transmit.

01

Read the demand

Read how well the output satisfies its regularizer and how much sample variation it already contains. Combine these into the demand q.

q = √(qreg qexc)

02

Measure the weaker supply

Measure sample variation in H and its transmission to Z. The weaker term determines the available supply k.

k = min{kexc, ktr}

03

Correct the encoder

When supply is below demand, apply the one-sided penalty directly to H. Once supply is sufficient, the extra penalty switches off.

S = [log(q + ε) − log(k + ε)]+2

ONE-SIDED CORRECTION

How the gate behaves

The penalty grows with the supply deficit. It is zero when supply reaches demand.
