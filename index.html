Abstract

We propose SADL (Supply And Demand Loss), which keeps the original training objective and constrains the encoder directly when needed. SADL compares the representation differences that the projector output demands with the differences the encoder can retain and pass on to the projector, and adds a constraint on the encoder only when supply falls short of demand.

It works with different Demand Regularizers and introduces no auxiliary networks, additional trainable parameters, or architectural changes. We evaluate SADL on LeWorldModel (LeWM) and INTACT in single-task and jointly trained four-task control, and examine its generalization to image classification.

MOTIVATION

A well-constrained projector output
does not by itself prevent representation collapse.

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) learn from images and video by predicting latent targets instead of reconstructing every pixel. An encoder maps inputs to a representation H, which a projector maps to an endpoint Z; existing Demand Regularizers typically constrain the geometry at Z.

In LeWM and INTACT, the encoder representation H is dominated by a component shared by all inputs from initialization. Batch normalization in the projector subtracts the batch mean. In training mode, adding the same vector to every row of H leaves Z unchanged, so neither the Demand Regularizer nor any loss computed from Z acts on the shared component of H.

The remaining variation grows mainly through the task gradient and concentrates in a few directions. The projector can remix and amplify existing variation to meet the Demand Regularizer's requirements, but cannot recover what H has lost.

On the shared direction, SADL supplies the nonzero gradient that the other losses lack; on the remaining directions, it lets the weaker ones grow faster.

METHOD

Demand and Supply Capacity

SADL reads a detached demand capacity q, measures the supply capacity k of H, and corrects H through a Supply Regularizer only when k < q.

01

Demand capacity

The demand capacity combines two terms from the detached endpoint: qreg increases as the Demand Regularizer is satisfied, while qexc measures the current sample variation at the endpoint. The demand capacity is the geometric mean of the two terms.

02

Supply capacity

SADL measures the supply capacity k as the weaker of the sample variation (excitation) in H and its transmission to Z. Hence k ≥ q requires both terms to reach q, so that even the smaller supply term meets the demand.

03

One-sided Supply Regularizer

We penalize insufficient supply capacity through a one-sided Supply Regularizer. It covers both excitation and transmission and is zero by construction whenever the supply capacity meets the demand capacity.

Training objective

L = Ltask+ λD D(Z)+ λS S(H, sg(Z))

The task loss and chosen output regularizer train as before. In the added term, sg stops gradients through Z, so the supply correction acts directly on H.

Adaptive demand

A constant demand must be re-tuned for each setting, whereas the adaptive q needs no tuning. Across five control and image settings, the adaptive q stays within seed variation of the best constant.

WORLD-MODEL CONTROL

Convergence and representation quality

We evaluate LeWM and INTACT on PushT, Cube, Reacher, and TwoRoom, reporting average control success across the four tasks.

Control success after one and two epochs

For both models and both backends, SADL after one epoch exceeds the baseline after two epochs. At epoch 2, INTACT + SADL reaches 96.3% mean success with either MATH or Flash.

General adaptation across
Demand Regularizers

We test SADL with eight Demand Regularizers rather than assuming a single Gaussian target. After one epoch, all eight give INTACT over 92% search-free Direct success; seven reach 95.03–95.81%.

IMAGE REPRESENTATION LEARNING

Generalization to image tasks

Beyond control, we evaluate SADL on ImageNet-LT, Galaxy10, CIFAR-10, and Imagenette under matched budgets and Demand Regularizers.

SADL improves early learning across the four datasets. Imagenette gains 8.91 percentage points at epoch 20; the gap narrows to 0.44 points at epoch 800. On CIFAR-10, the main benefit is early in training, with little change at the final epoch.
